abstract: >-
    LLMs excel at function- and file-level code generation but falter at the repository level, where the relevant context is scattered across many files and functions. Code context engineering tackles this through three paradigms—similarity-based in-context learning, static analysis, and navigation-based exploration—yet their individual contributions remain entangled within complex agents. This paper presents the first large-scale empirical study that compares the three paradigms independently across ten representative methods and eight LLMs, introducing a Dependency Collection Rate metric to expose clear trade-offs and offer actionable guidance for building context-aware coding tools.
